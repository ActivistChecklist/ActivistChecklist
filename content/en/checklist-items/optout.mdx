In the United States, you have the right to opt out of facial recognition when passing through security (TSA), and US citizens can also opt out at passport control (CBP). As of December 2025, [non-citizens (including green card holders) are now required to comply with facial recognition](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202511/dhs-finalizes-rule-expanding-biometric-entry-exit-to-foreign-visitors) when entering and leaving the US.

TSA claims it doesn't store your photo after it matches your face to your ID. But in 2026, a [DHS Inspector General report](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/dhs-inspector-general-finds-tsa-not-properly-safeguarding-passenger-facial) found that the company that supplies TSA's face scanners had pulled passport and driver's license images without TSA knowing. We have also seen many instances in the past where other facial recognition systems have experienced [massive data breaches](https://us.norton.com/blog/emerging-threats/biometric-data-breach-database-exposes-fingerprints-and-facial-recognition-data) and that could happen here. You gain nothing from allowing the government to scan your face at the airport/border.

<HowTo title="How to opt out of facial recognition at the airport">
  You can and should opt out of facial recognition every time it is offered:

  * Security screening (TSA, etc)
  * The gate where you're boarding (some airlines are using facial recognition at the gate)
  * Immigration and passport control (they have a camera to take your picture, but US citizens can opt out).

  It's easy to opt out:

  1. Simply say **"I want to opt out of facial recognition."**
  2. **Do not hand your ID over or put it in the scanner machine until the agent acknowledges your request.** Otherwise you may be locked into the facial recognition process.
  3. **Wear a mask** for further protection when going through the airport. If you're wearing a mask, you will need to take it down so the TSA agent can make sure your face matches the photo on your ID.
  4. If you're ever not sure what to do, just ask an agent nearby: "I'd like to opt-out of facial recognition, where do I go to do that?" They will often bring you to a separate line for manual screening (which sometimes gets you through faster!)
</HowTo>
