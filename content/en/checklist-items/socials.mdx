---
title: Hide or deactivate social media accounts
preview: >
  Helps protect you if they look up your social media accounts online (without
  needing your device)
---
Unless you're intentionally trying to maintain a widely public social media presence, it's a good idea to lock down your accounts at least temporarily. (Consider doing it long-term for more privacy and security. Or just deleting the accounts outright if you can manage it.)

**Visitors:** If you are applying for a tourist/business visa or traveling without a visa through ESTA, make sure you hide your social media before you apply. We know from [the case of an Australian journalist](https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/how-my-reporting-on-the-columbia-protests-led-to-my-deportation) that waiting until the week before you fly might mean they have already looked you up online.

**Student, work, and other visa applicants:** In June 2025, the State Department started [requiring all student and exchange visa applicants to make their social media profiles public](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2025/jun/18/social-media-student-visa-screening). Since then, they have added [many other visa types](https://eiglaw.com/dos-expands-social-media-screening-to-tn-td-and-i-visa-applicants/). The State Department [tells officers](https://www.presidentsalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/DOS-Cable-6.18.25-Social-Media-Vetting-and-Prioritization_Copy.pdf) that a private account "could be construed as an effort to evade or hide certain activity," and to take screenshots of what they find in case you delete it later. So for these visas, hiding your accounts can count against you. If this applies to you, you may want to actually delete old posts using a tool like [Block Party](https://www.blockpartyapp.com/), but talk to an immigration lawyer first and do it well before you apply.


<HowTo title="How to hide or deactivate social media accounts">
  Instructions:

  1. **X / Twitter:** [make it private](https://www.lifewire.com/make-your-twitter-profile-private-3486093) or [deactivate it](https://help.x.com/en/managing-your-account/how-to-deactivate-x-account)
  2. **LinkedIn:** [make it private](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a518980/) or [deactivate it](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340802/temporarily-disable-account)
  3. **Instagram:** [make it private](https://help.instagram.com/448523408565555) or [deactivate it](https://help.instagram.com/728869160569983)
  4. **Facebook:** [make it private](https://www.guidingtech.com/hide-all-posts-on-facebook/) or [deactivate it](https://www.facebook.com/help/214376678584711)
  5. **TikTok:** [make it private](https://support.tiktok.com/en/account-and-privacy/account-privacy-settings/making-your-account-public-or-private) or [deactivate it](https://support.tiktok.com/en/account-and-privacy/deleting-an-account/deactivate-your-account)
  6. Consider hiding other public posts like blog posts, YouTube videos.

  Consider using [Block Party](https://www.blockpartyapp.com/) to assist with this process. [Cyd.social](http://Cyd.social) can also help you make a local, private backup first.
</HowTo>

<Alert type="warning">
  While hiding your posts against the government might help you get into the US safer, we still need to keep speaking out publicly through posts, protests, and boycotts, because staying quiet might protect us individually but helps the authoritarian system we're fighting against.
</Alert>

If you're a US citizen, you are not obligated to provide your social media handles to CBP (Customs & Border Protection) or to the TSA. You can say that you are not comfortable answering or that you do not see the relevance of the question to your travel. Do not volunteer additional information for any question. (If you're on a visa or traveling through ESTA, refusing to answer can get you denied entry.)
