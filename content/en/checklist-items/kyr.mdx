<HowTo title="What are your rights when crossing the border">
  We have summarized some information below, but we strongly recommend you read these articles to familiarize yourself with the nuances.

  **Read the following articles to learn your rights:**

  * [Know Your Rights travel guide](https://stopaapihate.org/2025/05/19/know-your-rights-when-traveling/) from Stop AAPI Hate (2025)
  * [Is it safe to travel with your phone right now?](https://www.theverge.com/policy/634264/customs-border-protection-search-phone-airport-rights) from The Verge (2025)
  * [Can border agents search your electronic devices? It's complicated](https://www.aclu.org/news/privacy-technology/can-border-agents-search-your-electronic) from the ACLU (2025)

  <Alert type="info">
    **Border police CAN search any electronic device** at airports/borders without a warrant, regardless of your citizenship status. You have the right to decline to unlock your devices, but they may confiscate your device for weeks/months (Note: If you're a visa holder, they may deny you entry for failing to unlock your device).
  </Alert>

  **US Citizens:**

  * **Cannot be denied entry** to the US
  * **Must answer:** Identity and citizenship questions, and customs questions about what you're bringing into the country
  * **Can refuse:** Powering on, unlocking your devices, or giving your passcode. They can still search your bags, and they may take your devices and keep them for days, weeks, or months.
  * **Lawyer:** You can ask for one, but CBP doesn't allow lawyers during secondary inspection. (If they tell you that you're under arrest, ask for a lawyer and say that you're going to remain silent.)

  **Green Card Holders:**

  * **Cannot be denied entry** (without immigration judge hearing)
  * **Must answer**: Identity and permanent residency questions
  * **Can refuse**: Other questions and unlocking your devices (but you may face delays, have your devices taken, or face deportation proceedings)
  * **Don't surrender**: Your green card, even if pressured 

  **Visa Holders:**

  * **Can be denied entry** for refusing to unlock your devices or answer questions
  * **Must answer**: All border police questions about travel, background, and purpose if you want to enter.
  * **Device searches**: Refusal may result in visa revocation and deportation

  **If you're detained and questioned:**

  * **Never lie:** even small misrepresentations can lead to criminal charges
  * **Don't wipe your phone or try to hide anything once the search starts:** Do all your deleting and resetting before you travel. A case is still pending where an activist was [charged with a felony](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/07/activist-charged-with-felony-after-giving-border-agent-duress-code-that-wiped-his-phone/) after prosecutors say he gave border agents a special passcode that wiped his phone.
  * **Don't sign anything** you don't understand or haven't had a lawyer look at.
  * **Document everything:** officer names, badge numbers, what questions they asked you
  * **Get a receipt:** If they keep your device, ask for a receipt so you can attempt to track it down later.

  **Know the trade offs of not answering questions:**

  * While we would generally always suggest that you [don't talk to cops](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nWEpW6KOZDs), the situation at the border is more complicated. Citizens and non-citizens alike have fewer rights at the border.
  * If you're a US citizen, you can refuse to answer questions (besides the basics listed above), but it may result in long questioning and your device not being returned to you.
  * If you're a green card holder or visa holder, the risks are higher. Read the articles linked above to get a sense of your rights in those situations.
</HowTo>
