We are in an era where the federal government is surveilling and prosecuting activists with a level of intensity we haven't seen in decades.

When doing mass organizing, we're always balancing the need for security with the need to grow our movements and build power. 

One way we can reduce unnecessary risk is to be careful about what we write down. What we write down often ends up being used against us in court, even if we're using [encrypted](term:encryption) tools like Signal.

For any organizing involving risking arrest (or highly targeted organizing like [ICE watch](/ice/) or Palestine solidarity), it's important to have agreements about what folks will not write down.

How the data ends up in court:
* **Someone's phone is seized.** If anyone in a chat is arrested and police get into their phone, they can read every message and document on it.
* **A company hands over your account.** Services like Google Docs aren't end-to-end encrypted, so the company can see your documents and give them to police who get a [subpoena or warrant](term:warrant-subpoena). In 2025, Google gave ICE a graduate student's account information in response to a subpoena, [without telling the student first](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/04/google-broke-its-promise-me-now-ice-has-my-data).
* **Someone in the group is an informant or undercover cop.** In the 2026 federal case against the Minnesota 15, [undercover agents joined activists' Signal group chats](https://www.wired.com/story/ice-is-running-one-of-the-largest-anti-left-surveillance-operations-in-us-history/) and passed along what they read, which prosecutors then used in court.

<HowTo title="How to keep your plans out of the written record">
  Avoid putting riskier things in writing in messages, documents, or even paper notes.

  **Before you type/write something, ask yourself: "Would I be ok hearing this read aloud in a court case against me?"** If not, save it for an in-person conversation (ideally with all phones/devices in a different room).

  Things you may want to avoid writing down: 

  * That you are planning to do something that carries the risk of arrest (even a very mild sit-in is usually breaking a trespassing law)
  * Who is participating in the group or action
  * Who is leading
  * For actions: the location, the tactics, identifying information about people involved
  * Other groups involved
  * Jokes/venting/sarcasm that could read as a serious threat/intention.

  Last step: Build practices to **[delete/destroy things](/action/#action-cleanup) you did write down as soon as you no longer need them**. This often gets forgotten, so building in accountability systems can help.
</HowTo>

<Alert type="info">
  **Treat large or open groups as if an informant is in them.** You usually can't tell who is an informant. And it's bad practice to start trying to "find the snitch" in a group. The more people in a group, the harder it is to know everyone. Keep the most sensitive conversations to small groups of people you know and trust.
</Alert>
