We started Activist Checklist because we were seeing a lot of confusion and inaction around digital security practices in Palestine solidarity organizing. We began our research mid-2024 in anticipation that Trump might get elected again and that we'd see a rise in authoritarianism, state surveillance, and repression of social movements.

As activists ourselves, we understood the importance of protecting our data, and we wanted to make digital security more accessible.

## Why we built this site {#why}

**Looking around, we found that existing resources were either too simple or too complicated.**

Right before an arrestable action, we'd often get a list from the organizers of "things to do to secure your phone" that seemed incomplete and out of date.

On the flip side, the in-depth guides we found online went into so much detail that they became overwhelming for many.

We wanted to create a way for folks to achieve better security and privacy without needing to become experts themselves. This site is our attempt to offer comprehensive guides that don't make your eyes glaze over.

We also know that information alone isn't enough. Most people need accountability and support to actually follow through, which is why we encourage groups to [host a security party](/party/) and work the checklists together.

## Who this site is for {#who}

**This site is built for you if you are:**

* Doing [nonviolent civil disobedience](/action-safety/) (direct actions in public where you are knowingly risking arrest and you are not trying to remain anonymous).
* Doing work that is more likely to be targeted by the state or your opposition, like [ICE watch](/ice/).
* Newer to activism and organizing and want to build good security habits, even if your work isn't likely to be targeted.

**You probably need more strict security guides if you are:**

* Doing higher-risk direct action work that carries the risk of serious legal consequences.
* Trying to remain fully anonymous for a significant part of your political work.

If you need that higher level of security, you can check out [AnarSec](https://www.anarsec.guide/) and [NoTrace](https://www.notrace.how/resources/).

## Risk levels {#risk-levels}

Building a site that has static checklists is difficult because everyone's situations and needs are different. Ideally, every person would do their own [risk assessment (aka security plan)](https://ssd.eff.org/module/your-security-plan). Because we've seen many groups that don't have time or support to complete a risk assessment and make a security plan, this site attempts to give advice that can work in a variety of risk situations. It won't be perfect for everyone, but we hope it will increase the security baselines in our communities because people have an easier place to get started.

We include risk level boxes at the top of many sections to help you assess whether that section applies to you. We hope this will help you do a "mini risk assessment" in the moment so you know where to start. They look like this:

<RiskLevel level="everyone" />

<RiskLevel level="medium" />

<RiskLevel level="high" />

The specific meaning of "medium" and "high" varies depending on the context of each guide, so the explanation text may be different.

## Who built this site {#builders}

The crew that built this has been doing community organizing, activism, and direct actions for decades. We have been involved in organizing around Palestine, climate justice, Black Lives Matter, local mutual aid, anarchism, and more. Some of us do digital security work professionally in movement spaces, and all of us are doing local frontline work where we put it into practice.

We are currently choosing to remain anonymous in an attempt to preserve our own privacy given the risks involved in this kind of work.

Most of the work on this site happens on a volunteer basis. The project has a small budget from one grant we received. We use those funds to cover the server/infrastructure costs and occasionally to pay someone a stipend to help write a new guide if we can't find a volunteer to write it.

If you want to know how we protect this site and the people who use it, see our [security page](/security/) and [privacy page](/privacy/).

## How we write and review guides {#review}

Every guide goes through a review process to make sure it is as accurate as possible:

1. **Pick a relevant topic:** We pick our topics based on input from users and activists we're in community with. We try to address the most common questions/challenges we hear from folks.
2. **Draft a high-quality guide:** We find someone with a lot of experience and expertise to write a first draft. Sometimes that is someone on the core team, and sometimes it's someone else if we don't know the topic well enough.
3. **Peer review:** Every guide is reviewed by multiple folks who have extensive experience in that area. We integrate their feedback and do one or two more rounds of review to make sure the final result is clear and accurate.
4. **Ongoing maintenance:** We review the guides regularly, and we immediately update a guide when we hear about new security threats. If a guide is ever more than a year old, a notice shows up at the top of the page so folks know to double-check the information.

Our guides and pages are written by humans. We sometimes use AI to help with smaller tasks (like copy editing, drafting changelog entries, and writing SEO descriptions), and we review and edit 100% of that text. We also use AI for the first draft of translations and for most of the code behind the site. You can read more on how we do and don't use AI to build the site [here](https://github.com/ActivistChecklist/ActivistChecklist/wiki/How-we-do-and-don't-use-AI-in-building-the-site).

## Our sources {#sources}

We draw on a number of sources, and we evaluate them to assess how relevant and trustworthy they are for our audience.

Here are some of the sources we go to for recommendations:

* Electronic Frontier Foundation's [Surveillance Self-Defense](https://ssd.eff.org/) guide and [Street-Level Surveillance](https://sls.eff.org/) project
* [Freedom of the Press Foundation](https://freedom.press/digisec/)
* [Civil Liberties Defense Center](https://cldc.org/security/)
* [Digital Defense Fund](https://digitaldefensefund.org/)
* [PrivacyGuides.org](https://www.privacyguides.org/)
* Equality Labs' [Anti-Doxing Guide](https://www.equalitylabs.org/research/publications-resources/)
* Front Line Defenders' [Security In-A-Box](https://securityinabox.org/)
* Tactical Tech's [Holistic Security Manual](https://holistic-security.tacticaltech.org/)
* We track the latest surveillance news by following [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/), [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/technology/), [Citizen Lab](https://citizenlab.ca/publications/), [EFF](https://www.eff.org/), [Wired](https://www.wired.com/tag/security/), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/privacy), and many others. You can see some of the articles we're reading in our [surveillance news section](/news/).
