This guide will show you how to create and use an anonymous email account. Anonymous email accounts primarily protect personal privacy and hide the identity of the account-holder.

This guide is written for the following situations:

1. Protecting yourself from government and corporate surveillance.
2. Communicating online in un-vetted spaces where surveillance is likely to be used.
3. Whistle-blowing and investigative journalism.
4. Protecting yourself from harassment and doxxing.

This guide does **not**:

1. Shield the contents or metadata of emails obtained by law enforcement, whether through search warrants and subpoenas, or other means.
2. Stop your email provider from releasing this information about an email you own.
3. Protect your identity in the case that you share personal or identifying information about yourself online.
4. Protect your identity in the case that you use forms of payment that expose your identity, such as credit/debit cards or bank details.
5. Shield you from legal prosecution.

<Alert type="warning" title="encryption-vs-anonymity">
  Anonymous email is not the same as encrypted email. Anonymity hides the owner of the email address, while encryption hides the contents of a communication. While [Proton Mail](/proton/) can facilitate encrypted email, this is outside the scope of this guide.
</Alert>

<Section title="Using your anonymous email" slug="using-anon-email">
  <Alert type="warning">
    You must **never** forget this step. Logging into your anonymous email account without a VPN (or [Tor](/research/)) even **one time** could de-anonymize your account, potentially allowing law enforcement to link any of your account's activities to your identity. If it happens, abandon the address and create a new one.
  </Alert>

  <Alert type="warning" title="Your contents can de-anonymize you">
    Your email address is only anonymous as long as you don't use it to expose your identity, for example in your communications or by what you post online.
  </Alert>

  <Alert type="info" title="What Proton Mail encrypts">
    Emails sent between two Proton Mail accounts are end-to-end encrypted. Emails sent to non-Proton email addresses will be visible to the recipient's email provider. If the recipient's emails are obtained by law enforcement, they may be able to view your communications.
  </Alert>
