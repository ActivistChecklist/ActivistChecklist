**Who this checklist is for:**

* ✅ Anyone traveling **internationally** crossing the US border to enter or re-enter the country (whether by land, air, or sea).
* ✅ This checklist may also be applicable for **domestic** air travel within the US (especially if you are not a US citizen). On domestic flights, police generally need a warrant to search your phone (unless you agree to it). But ICE has been arresting people at airports (see below). For domestic flights, we think the most important steps are powering off your phone, using a strong passcode, and keeping your operating system up to date.
* ❌ This checklist is <u>**NOT**</u> **designed for people in the US who don't have US citizenship and are at risk of deportation.** If you need advice for flying as an immigrant with varying documentation statuses, see these resources: [Immigrants Rising](https://immigrantsrising.org/resource/guide-for-undocumented-individuals-traveling-in-the-u-s/), [National Immigration Law Center](https://www.nilc.org/resources/community-alert-immigration-arrests-at-airports/)

While we recommend the steps below for both entering and exiting the US, you are under much more scrutiny while ***entering the US***, so that is the time to be most cautious. If you're only going to follow these steps once, make sure it is when you enter the US.

We recommend completing the [Security Essentials](/essentials/) checklist as well.

<Alert type="warning">
  **ICE at airports:** In 2026, ICE appears to be detaining more people at airports, including people with open asylum cases and people on domestic flights. TSA [now shares passenger information with ICE](https://www.asianlawcaucus.org/news-resources/guides-reports/know-your-rights-at-airports), so ICE may know about your flight before you get to the airport. A few examples: [someone with a pending case to stay in the US, arrested while boarding a plane in Denver](https://abc7news.com/post/caught-video-ice-arrests-chantal-morales-rojas-oakland-california-woman-denver-airport/19577663/), [a failed detention in Las Vegas](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/07/16/police-ice-agents-walk-away-attempted-arrest-leaving-man-handcuffed-harry-reid-airport/), and [two detentions in Boston](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2026/07/18/2-arrested-at-logan-airport-as-ice-activity-ramps-up/).
</Alert>

## Short version of this guide

Either bring a secondary phone, backup and factory reset your device, or wipe everything sensitive off your device before you travel. Keep your phone off, disable [biometrics](term:biometrics), use a randomly generated passcode. Learn your rights. If you're not a US citizen, talk to an immigration lawyer before you travel. If you're applying for a tourist visa or traveling through ESTA, hide your social media before you apply (but note that for student, work, and other visas, hiding your accounts can count against you). If higher risk, get a legal team and have a check-in plan with trusted contacts.

<Section title="Baseline protections" slug="baseline">
  <RiskLevel level="everyone" />

  Most of the device searches we've seen have involved border police manually browsing a phone looking for things they don't like (criticism of the administration, support for Palestine, etc.). They usually tell you to unlock your phone and rely on your compliance. So before you travel, decide what you'll do if they ask you to unlock your phone:

  * **Bring only what you'd be OK with them seeing, and unlock it if they ask.** If you are not a citizen, this is probably your safest option, because you risk being sent back to your country of origin if you do not comply and unlock your phone. (A secondary phone or a factory reset phone is the safest way to do this. See the High-profile section below.)
  * **Refuse to unlock it (US citizens only).** If you are a US citizen, you do not have to power your phone on, unlock your phone, or give them your passcode. But they can still take it and keep it for days, weeks, or months. They'll have the hardest time breaking into it if it's fully powered off and has a strong passcode (see below).

  **Note - Deleting data doesn't mean it's gone:** This section suggests deleting apps and data. However, governments can use [forensic tools](term:forensic-extraction-tools) to access data after it has been deleted. So the guidance below offers reliable protection only against an adversary manually searching through your device, which is the most common situation at the US border.

  If you're at higher risk and think the cops might invest more resources to access the data on your phone/laptop, make sure to follow steps in the [High-profile protection](#high) section below in addition to what's here.

<Section title="High-profile protections" slug="high">
  <RiskLevel level="high" mode="for_you_if">
    * You are not a US citizen, or you became a citizen through naturalization.
    * You are doing activism, community organizing, or other work against the administration that puts you in a **position of power** or **public prominence**.
    * You are a public figure (artist, scientist, human rights lawyer) that has spoken out very publicly against the administration.
    * You have any other reason to think you might be more of a target than most folks crossing the border.
  </RiskLevel>

  This section assumes that border agents might go further and attempt to hack your device using [forensic extraction tools](https://sls.eff.org/technologies/forensic-extraction-tools). The best approach in this case is to bring a secondary device (sometimes called a "burner phone" or "travel laptop"). Because you can be denied entry or have devices seized indefinitely, your goal should be to travel with devices that you would be able to unlock and display to a border agent. If your device is ever taken out of your sight, it is usually best to assume they may have copied everything on it.

  These stories can be intense to read, but they can also help us understand the specific threats that people crossing the border are facing. Most of these people were targeted because their work was relatively high-profile.

  **Non-citizens:**

  * 2025 October - [Canadian man denied entry at the Port Huron border crossing after telling agents he was going to a No Kings protest](https://www.wgvunews.org/news/2026-03-27/lawmakers-question-border-treatment-after-canadian-man-denied-entry-in-port-huron). Agents searched his phone, fingerprinted him, and took a DNA sample.
  * 2025 June - [Australian independent journalist deported for his reporting on the Columbia Palestine solidarity encampments](https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/how-my-reporting-on-the-columbia-protests-led-to-my-deportation). Takeaways: lock down your social media and public posts before you apply for a visa, not before your flight.
  * 2025 March - [French researcher denied entry for being critical of Trump administration](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2025/mar/19/trump-musk-french-scientist-detained)

  **Citizens:**

  * 2026 September - [Immigrant rights organizer (a naturalized citizen) questioned about the books he was carrying at Miami airport, then pressured into handing over his phone](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/miami-immigrant-advocate-sues-federal-government-over-phone-seizure-40587572/).
