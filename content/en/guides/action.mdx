This guide covers digital security if you need to bring your phone to a nonviolent civil disobedience action (aka direct action) where some folks are planning to risk arrest. It's one part of keeping your crew safe. Your action should also plan for physical safety, have a safety team, and make sure everyone knows their rights.

<Alert type="warning">
  **Assess your risk level**: This guide is for actions where you are taking relatively lower legal risk (example: a sit-in or chaining yourself to a door). If you are planning something where you are striving for anonymity or where the legal consequences are much higher, you should be taking a lot more precautions than this guide offers (see [NoTrace.how](https://notrace.how)).
</Alert>

This guide is written for the following situation:

* This is an action that is centrally organized enough that you can set agreements around digital security that everyone will follow.
* You have encouraged most people attending the action to **not bring their phone**
* Some people still need their phones to coordinate your deployment (ie: tactical lead, medics, de-escalation team, etc.)
* The risk of arrest is high, or you are planning to risk arrest.
* You expect the police may try to use [phone-cracking tools](term:forensic-extraction-tools) to access the data on the phones they confiscate.
* You are not concerned with surveillance to and from the action.

If any of these don't apply to your action, you may need to take more precautions. Check out the [protest enhanced phone security section](/protest/) or consider bringing a [secondary phone](/secondary/) (sometimes called a "[burner phone](term:burner-phone)").

**Tip for organizers:** In the lead-up to the action, consider [hosting a security party](/party/) so your crew works through these steps together before the day of.

<Section title="Why does this matter?">
  We know social movements have been surveilled, infiltrated, discredited, and obstructed by government agents (local cops, FBI, CIA, NSA, etc) for decades. The rise of technology means we’re all being tracked and profiled (even if you've never been arrested).

  Even when we are exercising our legal rights to free speech and assembly, there are many government forces that seek to crush dissent and criminalize activists. (See [Minnesota 15 case](https://www.wired.com/story/ice-is-running-one-of-the-largest-anti-left-surveillance-operations-in-us-history/))

  **But there are actions we can take to protect our movements!** This guide offers some simple things we can all do before and during a protest/action to keep ourselves and the most vulnerable folks in our movements safer. Taking these precautions helps protect everyone you organize with (not just you and your data). It isn't a sign or admission of any kind of wrongdoing.

  ### Threats to consider

  * **Phone seizure and searching** - If arrested, police can try to break into your phone to access your messages, photos, and other private information to use as evidence
  * **Monitoring of communications** - Police can intercept regular SMS texts and calls to gather info about protest plans and participants
  * **Subpoena of your account data or cell carrier data** - Cops can get a court order to access your online accounts (like Google or Apple) or your communication/location data from your cell carrier.
  * **Mapping activist networks** - By getting data from one person's phone, police can identify entire communities of activists and organizers connected to them
</Section>
